Save · Lesson 2 of 3

Build a budget — and pay yourself first

Most people spend first and hope something is left to save. Usually there isn’t. Flip it: when money arrives, move savings out before you spend on anything else.

1Learn 2Try it 3Check

1Learn

  1. The 50/30/20 mapAbout 50% needs (phone bill, lunch, gas), 30% wants (games, clothes, going out), 20% savings. Your numbers can shift — the point is having a plan.
  2. Goals need numbersA goal without a number and a deadline is just a wish. “Save for a phone” becomes “Save $800 by March.”

How this works: “left after spending” is what’s free each month — you decide how much of it to save. Money you’ve already saved counts toward the goal, not as monthly income. We count whole months and round any partial month up, and round monthly amounts up to the next dollar. No interest is added: for a goal this close, keep the money somewhere safe like a savings account, not in investments. The 50/30/20 split above is one example, not a rule.

Remember

Saving isn’t leftover luck — it’s a decision you make first. Every dollar needs a job, especially the ones you save.

Quick check

You want a $600 item in 4 months. How much do you need to save per month?

$600 ÷ 4 = $150/month. A number plus a deadline turns a wish into a plan.
